# River Hub — onboarding the rest of England

*Implementation plan, 24 Nov 2026. Builds on the scoping note `NATIONAL-SCALING.md` (written before the
national site existed; its deployment-model question is now settled: one national site, `/r/<river>`)
and the per-river runbook `NATIONAL-ONBOARDING.md`, which stays the source of truth for how a single
river is loaded and verified.*

About **310 catchments remain** (some Severn/Dee ones are wholly in Wales and drop out). The EA annual
return lists roughly 14,500 storm overflows in England, so about 13,000 more overflows: around ten
times today's data.

What already works England-wide with no per-company code: the EA asset register and EDM annual return,
EA "Detailed EDM Start/Stop" spill events (PR #26), WINEP, EA water quality, EA rainfall/flow,
protected areas, ONS population, pollution incidents. The one per-company piece, the live "spilling
now" feed, is a National Storm Overflow Hub ArcGIS node per company, read by the same `arcgis_edm`
code path (config only, once both feed parsers handle the company's field casing).

## 2. Phase 0: make the platform ready (about 1–2 weeks)

Do this before the next company, so 340 rivers don't hit the limits one batch at a time.

1. **Data volume.** At ten times today the database would be roughly 15–20 GB.
   - Water quality (`ea_wq_samples`, 57% of the database): keep only the determinands and sampling
     points the pages use, or a recent window; decide with Rob (§6).
   - Hourly snapshots: keep the latest per outlet plus a daily summary; drop raw hourly rows after N days.
   - `spill_events`: check indexes and consider partitioning by year before ~10M rows.
   - Confirm the Supabase plan's included storage and compute against the target size.
2. **Live sync per company, not per river.** `/api/cron/edm-sync` fetches each river's feed separately
   (concurrency 5, ~17 s for 29 rivers). At 340 rivers: fetch each company's feed once (about ten feeds),
   then write each river's snapshots from it. Keep `?slug=` for one-off re-syncs.
3. **Catchments served by two companies.** Mainly the Welsh border (Severn Trent / Dŵr Cymru /
   Hafren Dyfrdwy) and some Thames/Southern/Wessex edges. A river needs more than one live feed:
   assign each outlet to its own company's feed rather than one `edm_feed_url` per organisation.
4. **Pipeline at scale.**
   - Generator: enumerate a company's catchments automatically (point-in-polygon of the company's
     outlets against the RBD's river opcats, as in runbook §7) and write all configs in one go.
   - Batch runner: parallel (e.g. 4 rivers at a time), continue on failure, and run the org-scoped
     `verify-river.sh` for every river as part of the batch, never trusting whole-table counts.
   - Name review sheet: each batch outputs opcat name → proposed river name / slug for approval
     (EA names like "Bristol Avon Little Avon Axe and North Somerset Streams" need tidying).
5. **National pages at 340 rivers.** Check and if needed adapt: home page and river chooser, England
   map (district bubbles and river dots), district pages and compare view, sitemap, `national_river_rollup`
   refresh time, OG image generation.

**Done when:** a dry run of one new company's enumeration + generation + a 3-river parallel batch
loads and verifies cleanly, the database projection fits the chosen plan, and the live sync handles
two companies' feeds in one pass.

## 3. Phases 1 to 8: one water company at a time (about a week each)

For each company:

1. **Recon:** find its NSOH feed endpoint, confirm field casing in **both** feed parsers
   (`src/lib/edm/sync.ts` and `scripts/import_catchment.py`; the Tone lesson), id format, annual-return
   sheet name, and its feed licence (CC BY 4.0 credit).
2. **Preset:** add the company to `COMPANIES` in `gen_catchment_config.py`.
3. **Pilot river:** one catchment end to end, checked against the EA annual return (top 15 outlets
   exact, EA bbox count equals ours) and against the company's live feed.
4. **Overnight batch** for the rest, behind `live=false`.
5. **Re-run sweep** of failed steps (transient EA 500s and dropped DB connections are normal and idempotent).
6. **Verify** every river (org-scoped counts, assets coded to parish + water body, render smoke test)
   and confirm earlier rivers are unchanged.
7. **Name review:** Rob approves the river names and slugs.
8. **Go live** as one reviewed batch (`update organisations set live = true where region = …`).

Suggested order, from closest to the proven method to the largest volumes:

| Phase | Company | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wessex Water (rest of South West + Bristol Avon) | Preset and feed already proven on the Tone; completes the South West district |
| 2 | Southern Water | Self-contained South East district, coastal and bathing-water heavy |
| 3 | Thames Water | Fewer but very large catchments; tests performance on dense urban networks |
| 4 | Anglian Water | Large area, many small catchments |
| 5 | Severn Trent + Dŵr Cymru + Hafren Dyfrdwy | Needs Phase 0 item 3 (two-company catchments) on the Welsh border |
| 6 | Yorkshire Water | Most of the Humber district |
| 7 | Northumbrian Water | Northumbria district |
| 8 | United Utilities | North West district, the largest overflow count |

## 4. Ongoing, once live

- **Annual reload** when the EA publishes the EDM annual return (each March): `edm-annual` for every river.
- **WINEP reloads**, then `match_winep_flows()` (migration 0103).
- **Health checks** across all rivers (extend `/api/cron/data-check` to report per-river gaps: no
  snapshots in 24 h, no spill events, zero assets).
- **Attribution:** a live-data credit per company (CC BY 4.0, confirmed per company in recon).
